## Workflow and state-machine design
Proposed lifecycle: `BUILT>SCANNED>STAGED>APPROVED>DEPLOYED|ROLLED_BACK`. Each arrow is a **candidate transition**, not permission to skip authorization. Terminal, exceptional and reversal states must be separately enumerated during review. Every transition stores actor, prior/new version, effective policy, reason, timestamp, correlation ID, and audit evidence. On timeout/partial outage: persist UNKNOWN/PENDING_INVESTIGATION where external effect is possible; reconcile against authoritative counterparty before retry. Reject stale aggregate versions and unauthorized cross-institution references.

## Permissions and maker-checker
Roles: requestor (prepare), approver (distinct principal for high-risk changes), operator (execute after approval), auditor (read immutable evidence), security administrator (manage privileges without transaction authority). Enforce RBAC+ABAC, institution, branch, product and transaction limits server-side. All approval decisions capture principal, policy, purpose, timestamp and immutable evidence. Prevent self-approval and privilege escalation.

## Financial/ledger and reconciliation boundary
**Invariant:** signed artifact and immutable deployment provenance. This context may not create or mutate customer monetary balances. Where financial consequences arise, issue an authorized command to the owning accounting/transaction context and await an evidenced outcome. Settlement finality cannot be inferred from an internal status update.

## Threats, AML, privacy and operations
Threats: IDOR, replay, privilege escalation, forged approvals, out-of-order events, stale policy, data exfiltration, duplicate effects and tampered audit trails. Controls: OIDC/MFA as risk requires, deny-by-default policy, SoD, signed/audited administrative actions, key-management boundaries, rate limiting, traceability and retention policies. Screen sanctions/AML/fraud at applicable workflow boundaries; do not invent regulatory thresholds. Metrics: command outcomes, exceptions, queue age, reconciliation breaks, retry counts, stale-version conflicts, unauthorized access, p95/p99 latency and audit delivery failures. Support playbooks must include unknown-outcome investigation and fail-closed escalation.
