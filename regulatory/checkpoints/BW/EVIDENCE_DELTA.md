- **Article 2:** credit, microfinance, payment institutions and supervised holdings are in scope. For first-category EMF networks, the apex entity has full obligations; affiliates apply Titles 2 and 4, and proportionate convergence to Titles 3 and 5. For EMF categories 2/3, the full regime is linked to deposits exceeding **10 billion FCFA for two consecutive years**; other category 2/3 EMFs have differentiated obligations. This is a source-transcribed historical clause, **not approved for executable classification** without current amendments and institutional facts.
- **Article 16:** data must be *available* in the CEMAC state of the institution's principal headquarters, including in continuity scenarios. Do **not** infer a universal prohibition of foreign processing or a complete lawful hosting design from this sentence alone. Obtain full continuation and country-level privacy/outsourcing interpretations.
- **Articles 26–29:** event/log monitoring, prompt incident detection and postmortems, containment/recovery/crisis exercises, annual vulnerability and penetration tests and governing-body reporting. These are institutional obligations; an untested software design does not satisfy them.
- **Article 30:** traceable notice to COBAC *without delay* for a potentially significant incident; disjunctive criteria include critical functions, 10% of clients/counterparties, 10% of operations, >24-hour unavailability, sensitive/classified data loss or leakage, gross financial gain/loss beyond 0.5% of core own funds with a five-million-FCFA minimum, or reputation-damaging public disclosures. The President of COBAC may adjust the criteria by instruction. Follow-up is described one week after declaration and a summary after one month. Exact operative calculation, denominator, instruction amendments and timekeeping require legal approval. **Never silently encode these figures into current production policy.**
- **Article 41:** reproduced operative text says effective **2025-01-01**, with **12-month transition** for existing credit/payment institutions and **18-month transition** for existing EMFs. The same report's narrative (printed p.79) calls **2026-01-01** and **2026-07-01** dates of entry into force. The most plausible explanation is transition-versus-commencement terminology, but this is **interpretation, not issuer clarification**. New entrants, holdings and institutional exceptions need separate analysis.
